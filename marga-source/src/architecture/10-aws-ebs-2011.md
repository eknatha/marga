# AWS EBS re-mirroring storm, April 2011
company: Amazon Web Services
date: 21 April 2011
modules: cloud, sre
calc: retries, capacity
source: AWS post-event summary | https://aws.amazon.com/message/65648/
summary: A network change cut storage nodes off from their replicas, and the nodes' own recovery used all the spare capacity.

## Before you read on
Every storage node keeps a replica of its data on another node. If many nodes lose contact with their replica at the same moment, what do they all try to do next, and is there enough free space for it?

## The system
EBS provides network-attached volumes for EC2 instances. Each volume is replicated to another node in the same availability zone. If a node loses its replica, it looks for another node with free space and creates a new copy, which is called re-mirroring. A control plane coordinates this and also handles requests such as creating volumes, across the whole region.

## Timeline as reported
- In the early hours, in US-East-1, engineers began a routine network upgrade to increase capacity in one availability zone.
- Traffic was shifted onto a lower-capacity redundant network by mistake instead of to the other primary router. The lower-capacity network was overwhelmed, and many nodes lost contact with their replicas.
- The network was fixed, but a large number of nodes tried to re-mirror at once. Free capacity ran out, so many volumes became stuck and could not serve data while they looked for space.
- The re-mirroring requests also overloaded the control plane for the region, which meant that even customers in other zones had trouble using EBS APIs.
- Recovery took days. AWS added physical capacity, and gradually recovered volumes. A small fraction of volumes, below 0.1%, could not be fully recovered.

## Root cause
A configuration error during a network change, followed by a recovery behaviour that had no limit on its own demand and had not been sized for a large-scale event.

## What changed
- AWS added spare capacity, changed re-mirroring to back off and avoid exhaustion, and improved the isolation of the control plane from zone problems.
- It improved its communication with customers and extended credits.

## Lessons learned
- Do not let a control plane operation depend on the capacity that it is trying to fix.
- Add back-off and limits so a re-mirroring storm cannot overwhelm the system.
- Use more than one zone, and test failover regularly.
- Tell customers early, and give clear status and credits.

## Trade-offs to weigh
- **Aggressive repair against gentle repair.** Quick repair reduces the window of risk, and can cause a storm.
- **Spare capacity.** Idle space is expensive and pays for itself on the day it is needed.
- **Zone isolation.** Keeping a zone's trouble inside the zone requires separating the control plane and shared services.
